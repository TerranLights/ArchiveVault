## Assignment

This is a settler sub-unit within the Left Coast settler culture, not an Indigenous unit. The founding population is the Russian-American Company at Sitka (1799–1804), followed by American rule from the 1867 purchase and the gold rush (Juneau 1880). The unit is a sub-unit of the Left Coast, and its zone is Sphere. The research must cover the founding population and date, the zone (Sphere), present-day composition, the framework that defines it, and the disagreements the source leaves open. No "laws" only; the unit is a culture, and the identity of the unit is the culture of the settlers plus the later layers.

## Target

As scoped in `Targets/Cascadia/00 - Scope.md` section 2, row 58: the settler population of the Alaska Panhandle. The unit's zone within the target is **Sphere**. Present-day population share: 72,279, 0.12% of the 61,141,109 total (2020 base). The unit is 23.3% Native (the census category "American Indian or Alaska Native") alone or in combination, 6.3% Asian, 5.7% Hispanic.

## Founding population and date

1799–1880:
- The Russian-American Company at Sitka: a post in 1799, the fortified New Archangel after the 1804 battle with the Sitka Tlingit, the informal capital of Russian America from 1808.
- American rule from the 1867 purchase.
- Juneau founded in 1880 after the Tlingit leader Kowee led the prospectors Harris and Juneau to the gold.
- Norwegian fishing settlement at Petersburg (1897).
- The Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act (1971) and Sealaska.
- Today 62.6% non-Hispanic white, 23.3% Native (the census category "American Indian or Alaska Native") alone or in combination, 6.3% Asian.

## Zones

**Sphere** (proposed as a sub-unit of S1; decision 4). The unit's zone is Sphere, not Core. The scope file notes that the frameworks index reads Alaska as Indigenous-majority, but the census shows a population 23.3% Native overall, 45–49% in Prince of Wales-Hyder, Yakutat and Hoonah-Angoon, and 19.4% in Juneau. This unit is the settler population of the Panhandle.

## Framework that defines it

**Woodard's text:** the Left Coast runs "from Monterey, California, to Juneau, Alaska" [scholarly: Woodard, read]. This unit is the Panhandle portion of the Left Coast, with a Russian-American Company founding layer different from the Yankee Left Coast founding. The unit's culture is a mix of Russian, American, and Indigenous elements, with the Norwegian fishing settlement at Petersburg as a later layer.

## Seams

1. **Left Coast / Far West:** the crest of the Cascades, the Coast Mountains and the Coast Ranges (in Alaska, this is the boundary between the Panhandle and the interior Yukon).
2. **Left Coast / British Columbia coast:** the international line on the water (the Strait of Juan de Fuca and Haro Strait) and the 49th parallel.
3. **The settler coast and the Indigenous north:** Haida Gwaii, the Nass and Skeena, Prince of Wales Island, Hoonah-Angoon and Yakutat are places where Native people are close to half the population or more and where treaty, title or self-government is strongest.

## Disagreements

- **The framework index says Alaska is "read as Indigenous-majority."** That does not fit the Panhandle (23% Native), and Woodard's text puts Juneau on the Left Coast. This unit is the settler population of the Panhandle, read with the Alaska target as Indigenous-majority at the culture-area level.
- **Yakutat's status:** the scope file marks Yakutat inside the Panhandle (decision 12: "Yakutat is inside"), with Yakutat being the Tlingit nation whose coast runs north of Glacier Bay. The Jetté-Cook line does not create a clear boundary.
