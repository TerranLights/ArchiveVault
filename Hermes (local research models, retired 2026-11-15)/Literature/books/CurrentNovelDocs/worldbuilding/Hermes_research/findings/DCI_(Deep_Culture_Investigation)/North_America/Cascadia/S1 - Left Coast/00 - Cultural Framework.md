## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- The Left Coast's original settlers were predominantly **Yankees** (Yankeedom), who arrived from the Northeastern U.S. and New England, hoping to "found another New England" on the Pacific coast.
- The Yankees had the best practical knowledge of West Coast conditions of any colonial power because of the **long-distance ship-borne fur trade** conducted by New Englanders alongside New France in the 18th–19th centuries.
- Missionary societies from Yankeedom (driven partly by **fear of Catholicism**) organized settlement expeditions.
- The Yankees created a **provisional government in the Oregon Territory in 1843** — a self-governing experiment.
- Within months, **Borderlanders** (from Greater Appalachia) arrived, **doubling the population of the Williamette Valley** and eventually outnumbering Yankees **15 to 1** on the ground (Borderlanders dominant in rural settlement and farming; Yankees dominant in towns and civic institutions).
- Later layers: **Gold Rush arrivals** (1848–1852, including many from Appalachia and abroad), **European immigrants** (19th–20th century), and **coastal elite Yankees** who maintained a cultural presence.

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- Woodard's frame for the Left Coast: "The coast blended the **moral, intellectual and utopian impulses of a Yankee elite** with the **self-sufficient individualism of its Appalachian and immigrant majority.**"
- The resulting culture: **"idealistic but individualistic"** — unlike the gold-digging lands of the interior and very similar to western Oregon and Washington.
- It "would take nearly a century for its people to recognize it, but it was a **new regional culture**, one that would **ally with Yankeedom** to change the federation."
- Core values: **egalitarian, utopian, moralistic, entrepreneurial, pragmatic, anti-hierarchical, anti-authoritarian in a decentralized way, community-minded but self-reliant.**
- The Left Coast's defining trait is **utopian idealism expressed through practical self-governance and environmental stewardship**; it is a culture of **doers, not just talkers.**

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- The Left Coast is the **entire Pacific coast strip** from Cape Mendocino (northern California) north to the Alaska Panhandle.
- Bounded east by the **interior basins of the Far West** (Central Valley, high desert) and **Sudcalifornia** (Baja peninsula).
- This means the Left Coast overlaps *no* other DCI unit entirely: the **Far West's** California Central Valley, Oregon interior, and Washington interior are a separate unit; **Sudcalifornia** is distinct; **Indigenous units** (Northwest Coast, California, Plateau) predate and overlay the settler territories in complex ways.

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- **Washington state:** 7.7M (2020); Seattle–Tacoma–Bellevue MSA dominant.
- **Oregon:** 4.2M; Portland MSA dominant; Willamette Valley as cultural core.
- **California:** 39.5M (2020); Los Angeles, San Francisco Bay Area, San Diego — the Left Coast's cultural spine is the **coastal megaregion**.
- The Left Coast's population is **>50 million** concentrated in the coastal strip plus the Puget Sound/San Francisco Bay corridors.

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With Yankeedom:** Cultural brothers/sisters; Left Coast is a "westward offshoot" with a distinct individualist-utopian blend. The two eventually "ally with Yankeedom to change the federation."
- **With El Norte:** **Southern California** is the seam zone: Yankees settled there and blended with El Norte's Mexican-Catholic framework (missions, ranchos, Catholic institutions). The so-called "Gulch" (downtown LA) is heavily El Norte; the rest of the coast is left-coast-adjacent.
- **With the Far West:** The eastern boundary is the **Cascade Range/Coast Ranges gap** — the rain shadow and the valley/desert interior. The Left Coast's utopian environmentalism meets the Far West's resource-extraction individualism head-on (e.g., the Columbia River dams, the California water wars).
- **With the Far North (B.C. coast):** The Left Coast extends into British Columbia, sharing the settler culture with the **British Columbia coast** unit (S2), but the Canadian portions (Vancouver, Victoria) have a distinct British institutional layer (Common Law, Canadian federalism) absent from the U.S. portions.
- **With Indigenous units:** The Left Coast's settlers largely **overlaid** existing Indigenous territories (Northwest Coast, California, Plateau), but the seams are **not clean lines**: the Coast Salish, Chinookan, and other groups retained substantial land and political authority in many coastal towns and river valleys. The modern seams are **demographic** (urban coastal vs. rural interior) and **political** (LA, Portland, Seattle as Left Coast anchors).
