## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- The first European-established settlement layer on the BC coast was the **Hudson's Bay Company (HBC) fur-trade network**, oriented toward **coastal trade** (the NBC and the coast salmons).
- HBC posts (Fort Victoria, Fort Langley, etc.) were established in the 1840s–1850s as trading forts, not agricultural settler colonies.
- The real settler wave came after **the 1858 Fraser Canyon Gold Rush**, when the Colony of Vancouver Island and then the Colony of British Columbia were proclaimed (1858), and thousands of miners and merchants poured in.
- **Britain** layered its institutional shell (Common Law, parliamentary system, municipal incorporation) over the HBC trade network and the Indigenous population.

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- The BC coast's governing frame is **British institutional layering over Indigenous Northwest Coast culture**.
- Unlike the U.S. Left Coast (Yankee utopianism over Appalachian individualism), the BC coast is a **British colonial overlay** on a dense, culturally rich Indigenous substrate.
- The culture that formed: **British institutional confidence** (town governance, municipal incorporation, port city planning) + **Indigenous Northwest Coast social organization** (lineage-based, potlatch-driven, crest-bearing, totem-pole-bearing).
- The seam is **not a contradiction** but a **stack**: Indigenous social hierarchy (name, title, crest) + British civic hierarchy (ward, council, mayor). The modern culture is the negotiated product of both.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- The BC coast unit is the **coastal strip of British Columbia** (including Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and the mainland coast from just south of Prince Rupert to the Alaska Panhandle).
- Bounded east by the **Interior** (Far West-adjacent), west and north by the Pacific Ocean and Alaska.
- This is a **narrow, linear unit** — the coastal corridor is the only densely settled part; the interior is Far West, the Yukon border is Far North.

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- British Columbia population: 5.0M (2020).
- The coastal urban core: **Vancouver** (2.6M MSA), **Victoria** (0.4M MSA), plus the **Lower Mainland** (Burnaby, Surrey, Richmond, etc.) as a single conurbation that is technically part of the Vancouver MSA.
- The **west coast of Vancouver Island** and the **Haida Gwaii** and **Queen Charlotte Islands** are sparse, mostly Indigenous and small-town (Port Hardy, Port Alice, Masset, Skidegate).
- The **Alaska Panhandle border** (Juneau, Wrangell, Petersburg, etc.) is a small, sparsely populated fringe.

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With the Left Coast (S1):** The BC coast unit is culturally and institutionally distinct from the U.S. Left Coast: British vs. Yankee; Canadian federalism vs. U.S. federalism; but they share the **coastal settler frame** (port cities, fishing, forestry, trade).
- **With the Far West (S3):** The east boundary is the **Coast Ranges interior gap** — the only connection is the **Cariboo Road / Trans-Canada Highway** corridor through the Fraser Canyon and the Thompson River valley.
- **With the Far North (I6):** The Dene peoples of the BC interior and northern BC overlay and interweave with the coastal unit at the **Prince Rupert / Alaska Panhandle border**; the **Tsetsaut** (extinct) and other Athabaskan groups occupy the northern reaches.
- **With Indigenous Northwest Coast (I1):** The **Nuu-chah-nulth, Kwakwaka'wakw, Haida, Coast Salish, Tsimshian, Haisla, Oweekeno, Gitxsan, Nisga'a** all live within the unit's area; the settler culture is the **overlying layer**, not a replacement.
