## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- El Norte's first European-established layer was the **Spanish mission system** (1769–1823): **21 missions** (Alta California) plus the **Jesuit mission colony in Baja California** (1697, under the Jesuits; the first permanent mission was **Misión Nuestra Señora de Loreto Conchó**, founded in 1697 by Father Juan María de Salvatierra).
- The mission system was the **primary institution** — it was **not just religious** but also **agrarian, military, and political**: it converted Indigenous peoples, taught them Spanish and European trades, and tied the territory to the Spanish Crown.
- After Mexican independence (1821), the missions were **secularized** (1833), and the mission lands were granted to **Californio rancheros** as **ranchos** — the **Californio landed gentry** emerged.
- The **U.S. annexation** (1848, Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo) and the **Gold Rush** (1848–1852) brought the **Yankee and Appalachian settlers** who would blend with El Norte in the coastal strip.

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- Woodard's frame: El Norte is a **settler culture with a distinct Spanish-Mexican-Catholic framework**: **mission-centric**, **rivercrossing and ranching**, **shamanic and Franciscan networks**, **Californio** (the mixed-blood elite).
- El Norte's defining trait: **the mission system and its legacy** — the **Californio rancheros**, the **Catholic institutional layer** (churches, schools, charities), and the **geographic and cultural border with the Anglo world to the north**.
- The culture that formed: **a hybrid of Indigenous (Yuman, etc.), Spanish, and Mexican** traditions, with the **Californio elite** bridging the two; the **American settlers** (Yankees, later) overlaid this and **blended with El Norte** in the coastal strip (Southern California).
- The core value: **personal honor, religious devotion, landownership, and family/clan networks** — with a **younger, more aggressive, more masculine** rowdy streak (the "cattle-ranch 강국" and the "cowboy" culture) alongside the **mission's moral order**.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- El Norte is the **southern coastal Pacific corridor**: from **San Diego** (U.S.) north to **Los Angeles**, and across the **peninsula of Baja California** (the Mexican portion).
- Bounded east by the **Far West** (the Mojave and the Colorado Desert's western edge), west by the Pacific Ocean, north by the **Californio/American seam** (the historic boundary of the Mexican California province).
- **Sudcalifornia** (S5) is a **distinct unit** — the **northern Baja peninsula** (the Jesuit mission zone, the Gulf coast, the interior desert) — but they share the **Spanish-Mexican framework**.

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- The U.S. portion (Southern California): **18M+** (LA, San Diego, Riverside-San Bernardino) — the densest population of any unit in Cascadia, with an **urban-rural seam**.
- The Mexican portion (Baja California): **3.5M+** (Tijuana, Mexicali, Ensenada) — the **border economy** (maquiladoras, tourism, agriculture) is the defining feature; **Tijuana** is the largest city.
- The El Norte population is **overwhelmingly urban** (LA and Tijuana are both megacity-adjacent), with **rural ranchos and missions** as the historical core.

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With the Left Coast (S1):** The seam is **San Diego to Santa Barbara** — the coastal strip where the **Yankee elite** blended with the **Californio/Mexican** framework. The "Gulch" (downtown LA) is heavily El Norte; the rest of the coast is Left Coast-adjacent.
- **With Sudcalifornia (S5):** The seam is the **historic Californian province border**: the **Palóu Line** (roughly the boundary between the two Spanish colonial provinces) and the **Californio/American** boundary. The two units share the **Spanish-Mexican-Cahuilla framework** but differ in **U.S./Mexican jurisdiction** and **mission vs. ranch wealth**.
- **With the Indigenous units:** El Norte's **Yuman peoples** (Tipai, Kumeyaay, Cocopa) are the **original inhabitants** of the region; the **Californio** and **American** layers are **overlaid**, and the seams are **demographic** (the reservations vs. the cities).
