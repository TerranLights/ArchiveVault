## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- The Far West's first major settler layer was the **Gold Rush of 1848–1852** (the "Forty-Niners"): a **transient, extractive, male-dominated population** of miners, adventurers, and speculators from across the U.S. and abroad (though the scope notes that the fictional unit is set with mostly **European** settlers, with East Asian immigrants arriving later in the agricultural and railroad phases).
- The Gold Rush **discovered** the resources (gold, fertile soil) and **triggered** the settlement wave, but the real culture was **built later** by the **agricultural and industrial developers**.
- The **Transcontinental Railroad** (completed 1869) was the decisive infrastructure: the **Central Pacific** (later Southern Pacific) built eastward from California, and the **Union Pacific** crossed the continent, making the Far West's interior accessible and transforming it from mining to **agriculture, timber, and resource extraction**.
- The **railroad companies** (Central Pacific, later Southern Pacific) were the **most powerful institutions** in the Far West's interior, wielding more power than state governments (the "robber baron" era).

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- Woodard's frame: the Far West is a **resource-extraction culture** — a place of **mines, timber, wheat, and cattle** — and its defining institution is the **corporation** (mining companies, railroad companies, timber companies, agricultural syndicates).
- The Far West's culture is **pragmatic, self-reliant, hostile to "eastern" authority**, and **rooted in the land and its resources** (not in utopian ideals like the Left Coast, and not in lineage/ritual like the Northwest Coast Indigenous cultures).
- The Far West is a **culture of doers and extractors**: people who **work the land** (mining, farming, logging) and **build the infrastructure** (railroads, dams, roads, towns).
- The core value: **utility and material success** over "intellectual" ambitions; a **settler's pragmatism** combined with **entrepreneurial individualism**.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- The Far West unit is the **interior of the Cascadian target**: the California Central Valley, the high desert and basin-and-range country (the Mojave-adjacent regions, the Transverse Ranges' eastern fringes), the Oregon interior (the Columbia Plateau and the Willamette Valley's eastern fringes), and the Washington interior (east of the Cascades, the Columbia River basin and the Yakima Valley).
- Bounded by the **Left Coast** (to the west, across the Coast Ranges), **Sudcalifornia** (to the south, across the peninsula and desert), and **Indigenous units** (Plateau to the east; California peoples to the south).

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- The Far West's population is **distributed along the transportation corridors**: the **Central Valley** (California's agricultural heartland), the **I-5/I-84 corridor** (Oregon), and the **Columbia River basin** (Washington's irrigated high desert and the Yakima Valley).
- The population is **mostly white** (with the scope noting **East Asian immigration** in the later agricultural and railroad phases), with **smaller Indigenous populations** (Plateau and Great Basin edge peoples) in the eastern reaches.
- The Far West's population is **growing** (the Central Valley is one of the fastest-growing agricultural regions in North America; the Pacific Northwest's interior is growing with the Columbia River dam projects and the high-tech spillover from the coastal cities).

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With the Left Coast (S1):** The western boundary is the **Coast Ranges** and the **rain shadow gap** — the Left Coast's utopian environmentalism faces the Far West's extractive pragmatism head-on (the **California water wars**, the **Columbia River dams**, the **agricultural water disputes**).
- **With Sudcalifornia (S5):** The southern boundary is the **peninsula and desert** — the Far West's agriculture (Central Valley) meets Sudcalifornia's mining and tourism (Baja).
- **With the Indigenous units:** The Far West's interior is **predominantly the territory of the Plateau peoples** (I2) and **Great Basin edge peoples** (I5); the settler culture is the **overlying layer**, and the seams are **demographic** (the reservation counties vs. the corporate towns).
