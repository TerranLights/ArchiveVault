## Findings
- Sensuality in Cascadia is expressed through the region's natural beauty and the physical pleasures of the landscape. The "Beautiful Cascadia Region" description emphasizes "redwoods, rushing rivers, and awe-inspiring animals" as "the emblems of a bioregion—defined by natural characteristics such as topography and climate." "The beautiful bioregion woos nature lovers with redwoods, rushing rivers, and awe-inspiring animals." This suggests that a sensual appreciation of the natural world is a core Cascadian value.

- The landscape of Cascadia is a source of physical pleasure and aesthetic appreciation. "Fog shrouds towering evergreens, clouds catch on snowcapped volcanic peaks, and fish-filled rivers roll toward the Pacific." "They're the emblems of a bioregion—defined by natural characteristics such as topography and climate, not by borders people draw on maps—that encompasses the West's coastal ranges as well as the Columbia River's vast watershed." "It even has a name: Cascadia." "Embracing 600,000 square miles of some of the wildest beauty on the continent, Cascadia is united by its rivers and waterfalls, its volcanoes and vast mountain ranges, its wildlife (including bears, orcas, and elk) and its indigenous human cultures." The McKenzie River is described as "Cold, clean, crystalline water is a Cascadian specialty."

- Urban Cascadia is characterized by "a place that is geographically peripheral, aesthetically and politically green, and selfcontentedly different." "On the ground, urban Cascadia is obviously green." "Visitors frequently comment they have never seen such intense, abundant green before." "The verdant landscape owes much to steady if typically light precipitation through the winter months, and a temperate climate year-round." "Evergreen forests – although routinely clearcut – are the natural terrain of this region." "Nature literally looms large in these cities, with mountains (including active volcanoes) and significant bodies of water punctuating the green terrain visible in Portland, Seattle, and Vancouver." "The natural environment – whatever its actual color – is a frequent point of reference in Cascadian cities, from pedestrian commentary about how 'the mountains are out' (meaning the weather is clear enough for the highest summits to be visible), to widespread participation in wilderness-based recreation, to policies that actively seek to conserve or revitalize resources."

- The sensual appreciation of nature is also expressed through recreation and engagement with the landscape. "These features comprise the stripes of Cascadia's unofficial tricolor flag, with green, white (for snowcapped peaks), blue (for sea and sky), and an archetypal Douglas fir as centerpiece." "The natural environment – whatever its actual color – is a frequent point of reference in Cascadian cities, from pedestrian commentary about how 'the mountains are out' (meaning the weather is clear enough for the highest summits to be visible), to widespread participation in wilderness-based recreation, to policies that actively seek to conserve or revitalize resources whether in terms of recycling or limiting urban sprawl or promoting public transport."

- The Columbia Gorge is described as "the spiritual heart of Cascadia" with "the two defining landforms of the Northwest – the Columbia River and the Cascade Mountains – come together as one." "If falling waters define the region, then the dense gathering of waterfalls in the Gorge makes it the spiritual heart of Cascadia." "The eastern end, golden brown and nearly treeless, is the start of the arid inland Northwest. The western end manifests the very soul of Pacific Cascadia, lush, magical forests dressed in mist and moss."

- Cascadian culture has a strong connection to nature that is also spiritual. "Cascadia: The Elusive Utopia" notes that "Cascadia – repeatedly recognized in the collection as concept and dream rather than as mappable territory – is identified by the relative absence of affiliation to the institutional church and a strong sense of allegiance to a spirituality discovered in non-human nature." "In this secular but spiritual world we find reiterated in various forms the importance of rivers (though not much, oddly, on cascades), salmon, and proximate mountains; coffee and fleece also take a turn." "For Drury sustainability might be found simply in 'contemplative walking' (151) which is deeply spiritual while simultaneously honouring nature." "Cascadians are concerned for the integrity of the natural world, inclined to a slightly separatist scepticism about tradition, and in love with novel freedoms."

- Food culture is a key expression of sensuality in Cascadia. "A year in simple, seasonal Pacific Northwest cooking" emphasizes "eating in season in the Pacific Northwest" and "how different it is to eat only what's fresh from local farms and what she grows herself." "All the heirloom fruits and vegetables that you find at the farmers' markets, they have such a variety of colors and flavors that you just don't get if you don't shop seasonally." "It tastes so bonkers good when you eat an in-season vegetable as opposed on a not-in-season one." "When I moved back up here four years ago, it was a refresher on eating seasonally in the Northwest. I fell back in love with how nature provides you with what your body is craving at that moment."

- The "15 Essential Foods of the Pacific Northwest" describes an "obsessive, dedicated interest in seasonal and local cooking." "The region's expansive farmland, wild natural spaces, and generally stable climate make it an all-access ingredient theme park." "Potatoes are rolling out of Idaho at an alarming rate, salmon leap out of Washington rivers into burgers, sashimi, and whippy pink piles of mousse, and Oregon berries are almost impossible to avoid when they're in season." "People here lose their shit over wild mushrooms, trudging through muddy forests in search of sunny chanterelles and puckered morels, and Washingtonians will drive silly distances to pick their own apples or pears — the state grows the most of both in the U.S." "Trips to the coast marked with pit stops for salmon candy, tofu-based 'egg salad' sandwiches packed in lunch boxes; festival snacks of hot dogs dipped in pancake batter or slathered with cream cheese."

- The cultural value on seasonal eating and local food reflects a sensual appreciation of nature's rhythms. "By going to the farmers market, you're pretty much forced to cook seasonally, right? Because you're buying local stuff. And when you cook seasonally, you cook, I think, better food." "There are other advantages—like supporting small businesses and eating food that is less harmful to the environment."

- Mark Musick "transformed the way Seattle thinks of local, seasonal food" and "oversaw its first Community Supported Agriculture program, essentially a seasonal fruit-and-vegetable subscription, and introduced Organic Farmer days." "He's been 'one of the pivotal leaders in creating an organic/locavore food culture' in the Northwest." "Moving on to Pike Place Market, historic Ground Zero for locally farmed foods in Seattle, Musick was tasked with rebuilding the Market's role as a shopping destination rather than a tourist attraction."

- Sensuality in Cascadia is also expressed through the region's "temperate climate year-round" and "steady if typically light precipitation through the winter months." "Evergreen forests" and "lush, magical forests dressed in mist and moss" provide a rich sensory environment. "The air, soil, and waterways of Cascadia are defined by water. The vegetation's rich, fragrant."

- The Cascadian appreciation of physical beauty and sensory experience is balanced with a spiritual and environmental ethic. "Cascadians are concerned for the integrity of the natural world, inclined to a slightly separatist scepticism about tradition, and in love with novel freedoms." "For Drury sustainability might be found simply in 'contemplative walking' (151) which is deeply spiritual while simultaneously honouring nature." This suggests that sensuality in Cascadia is expressed through a respectful, appreciative relationship with the natural world rather than through exploitation.
