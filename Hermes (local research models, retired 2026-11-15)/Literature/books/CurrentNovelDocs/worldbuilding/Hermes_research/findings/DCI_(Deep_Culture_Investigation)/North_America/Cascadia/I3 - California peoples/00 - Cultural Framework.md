## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- The **California peoples** are **among the most diverse** Indigenous populations in **North America** — at the **time of European contact** (1769), the **population** was estimated at **340,000+** people, organized into **10 different linguistic families** with **at least 78 distinct languages** — the **highest language density** of any region in **North America**.
- The **peoples** are **organized into** **sedentary and semi-sedentary villages** of **400–500 people** (the "micro-tribes"), with **no** **centralized political authority** (the **chief** was **a ceremonial leader**, not a **political ruler**).
- The **Northern** (the **Yurok**, **Wiyot**, **Karuk**, **Hupa**, **Chilula**, **Tolowa**, **Mattole**) peoples are **adjacent** to the **Northwest Coast** and **shared** many of its **traits** (the **plank houses**, **dugout canoes**, **salmon fishing**, **ceremonial cycles**, **dentalium currency**, **the use of** **the **Northwest Coast** **cultural** **area**).
- The **Central Valley** peoples (the **Miwok**, **Ohlone** (Costanoan), **Maidu**, **Wintu**) are **different** from the **North Coast** — they are **more** **sedentary** (the **Ohlone** were **acorn** and **seed-grinding** specialists), **with** **larger villages** and **a** **more** **complex** **trade network** (the **Miwok** were **traders** in the **Central Valley**).
- The **Southern Coast** peoples (the **Chumash**, **Gabrieleño**, **Acjachemem** (Luiseño), **Cahuilla**) are **different** again — they are **more** **technologically advanced** (the **Chumash** were **the only** **California** peoples **outside** the **Northwest Coast** **to build** **plank boats** (the **tomol**), the **only** **others** to **use** **knotted** **cordage** and **the** **pipe**), and they are **the** **only** **others** to **use** **the** **twine**, **the** **net**, and the **basket** (the **Chumash** were **skilled** **basket makers**).
- The **Mojave** and **Colorado Desert** peoples (the **Mohave**, **Piipaash** (Maricopa), **Queuechan**, **Cocopah**) are **different** again — they are **river** and **desert** peoples, **more** **like** the **Great Basin** peoples **than** the **Central Valley** peoples.

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- The **California** peoples' **governing frame** is a **complex of **ecological** and **linguistic** **diversity**: **no single** "California culture" — **each** village or band was **a** **distinct** **people** with its **own** **language**, **subsistence**, **social organization**, and **spiritual** **traditions**.
- The **common** **threads** that **bound** the **California** peoples **together**: **a** **deep** **connection** to **the **land** and **its** **resources** (the **acorn**, the **seed**, the **salmon**, the **marine mammals**, the **grasslands**, the **desert**), **a** **kinship-based** **social** **organization** (the **band** and **the **family**), and **a** **spiritual** **tradition** that **was** **rooted** in **the **land** and **the **sacred** **plants** and **animals**.
- The **mission period** (1769–1833): the **Spanish missions** transformed the **California** peoples' **lives** — the **missions** were **not just** **religious** **outposts** but **agrarian**, **military**, and **political** **institutions** that **converted** and **sedentarized** the **Indigenous** **peoples**, **taught** them **Spanish** and **European** **trades**, and **disrupted** their **traditional** **subsistence** and **social** **organization**.
- The **mission period** **was** **violent** and **oppressive** — the **Indigenous** **peoples** were **forced** **into** **the** **missions**, **were** **taught** **Christianity** **and** **forbidden** **from** **practicing** **their** **traditional** **beliefs**, **and** **were** **subjected** **to** **harsh** **labor** (the **cattle** **grazing**, **the** **plowing**, the **building** of **the** **adobes**), with **cultural suppression** (the **ban** on **traditional** **ceremonies**, **languages**, and **spiritual** **beliefs**) being **commonplace**.
- The **California genocide** (the **19th**–**20th** **century**): the **Indigenous** **peoples** **of** **California** **were** **massacred**, **farmed**, **boiled** in **the** **missions**, and **forced** **into** **boarding** **schools** — **the** **population** **crash** **from** **340,000+** **to** **fewer** **than** **100,000** **by** **1900**; the **Indigenous** **peoples** **were** **not** **just** **affected** **but** **largely** **destroyed** **as** **a** **cultural** **entity**.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- The I3 unit is the **entirety** of **California's** **Indigenous** **territory** — the **coastal** and **inland** **regions** of **California**, from the **Oregon border** to the **Mexican border**, and **from** the **Pacific Ocean** **to** the **Sierra Nevada** and the **Mojave Desert**.
- Bounded by the **Northwest Coast** (to the **northwest**), the **Plateau** (to the **northeast**), the **Great Basin edge** (to the **east**), and the **settler units** (the **Left Coast** and the **Far West**).

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- The **Indigenous population** of **California** today is **weathered** — the **population** **crashed** from **340,000+** **to** **fewer than** **100,000** **by** **1900** (**the** **genocide**), and has **recovered** **to** **an estimated 500,000–800,000** today (the **scope file** gives the **exact** **number**).
- The **peoples** are **politically organized** into **tribe/band** governments (the **Yurok**, **Karuk**, **Hupa**, **Wiyot**, **Chumash**, **Gabrieleño**, **Acjachemem** (Luiseño), **Cahuilla**, **Miwok**, **Ohlone**, **Maidu**, **Wintu**, **Mono**, **Pomo**, **Salinan**, **Yokut**, **Kumeyaay**), with **land claims** and **self-government** agreements being a **major political feature** of the region (the **Yurok** and **Karuk** **land claims** **agreements**, the **Ohlone** **recognition** **efforts**, the **Cahuilla** **self-governance**).
- The **Indigenous population** is **concentrated** in the **rural** **communities** **and** **reservations** (the **Yurok** and **Karuk** **reservations** on the **Klamath** and **Trinity** rivers, the **Cahuilla** reservation in **Southern California**, the **Miwok** and **Ohlone** **rural communities** in the **Central Valley**), and **in** the **urban** **centers** (**the** **Indigenous** **urban** **population** **of** **Los Angeles**, **San Francisco**, **Sacramento**, and **San Diego**).

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With the Northwest Coast (I1):** The **North Coast** (the **Yurok**, **Wiyot**, **Karuk**, **Hupa**) is **the southernmost expression** of the **Northwest Coast** **cultural area** (per **Kroeber** 1939), **sharing** **many** **traits** (**plank houses**, **dugout canoes**, **salmon fishing**, **ceremonial cycles**, **dentalium currency**), but **differing** in **language** (**the Yurok and Wiyot are Algic**; **the Karuk and Hupa are Hokan and Athabaskan**).
- **With the Great Basin edge peoples (I5):** The **Mojave** and **Colorado Desert** peoples **border** the **Great Basin** **Indigenous** **peoples** **at** the **southern edge**, and **share** **a** **dependency on **river** **and **desert** **resources** (the **Colorado River** and the **desert**).
- **With the settler units (S1, S3):** The **California** peoples are **overlaid** by the **settler units** (the **Left Coast** and the **Far West**), but the **seams** are **not clean lines** — the **reservations** are **co-located** with the **settler towns** (the **Yurok** reservation on the **Klamath** near **Crescent City**), and the **Indigenous** population is **present** in the **urban centers** (**the Indigenous population of **Los Angeles**, **San Francisco**, **Sacramento**, and **San Diego**).
