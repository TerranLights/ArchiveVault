## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- The Northwest Coast peoples are **some of the oldest continuously inhabited cultures** in the Western Hemisphere — their territories have been occupied since the **last glacial maximum** (c. 15,000–12,000 years ago), with the **coastal migration** along the Pacific coast being the dominant theory of how humans first entered the Americas.
- The **cultural area** is the **narrow coastal belt** from **Yakutat Bay** (Alaska) to **Cape Mendocino** (northern California), bounded by the **Coast Ranges** to the east and the **Pacific Ocean** to the west.
- The **peoples** (listed from north to south): **Tlingit** (Alaska/Southwest Yukon), **Haida** (Haida Gwaii), **Tsimshian** (Nass and Skeena rivers), **Nisga'a** and **Gitksan** (interior Nass and Skeena), **Haisla**, **Heiltsuk**, **Kwakwaka'wakw** (Vancouver Island west coast), **Nuu-chah-nulth** (Vancouver Island west coast, including Nootka Sound), **Coast Salish** (the largest group: Central Coast Salish and Northern Coast Salish, spanning the Strait of Georgia, southern Vancouver Island, Puget Sound, the Olympic Peninsula, and the lower Columbia).

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- The Northwest Coast's governing frame is **maritime and salmon-based**: the culture is **defined by the sea and the rivers**, with a **heavy reliance on salmon, sea mammals, shellfish, and wild plant foods**.
- The culture is **lineage-based and ranked**: **noble lineages** (clans), **chiefs**, **name and title inheritance**, **crests**, and **potlatch** as the **central institution** — the **potlatch** is a **highly complex event** where a **prominent host** gives **expensive gifts** (food, blankets, masks, copper plates) to **guests**, and by **accepting** those gifts, the guests **validate** the host's **status, wealth, and rights**.
- The **potlatch** is also a **record-keeping system**: the **host's family history, rights, and privileges** are **publicly displayed** and **committed to the collective memory** of the **potlatching community** (through **totem poles**, **masks**, **houses**, **feast dishes**, and **oral tradition**).
- The culture is **highly productive**: the **coastal and riverine environment** is so **rich** that it **sustained dense populations** without **agriculture** — the **salmon runs**, **marine mammals**, **shellfish**, and **root crops** (the **camas** and **bitterroot** of the adjacent interior) provided **all** the **nutritional needs**.
- The **art** (totem poles, masks, canoes, weaving, jewelry) is **functional and symbolic**: it is **not art for art's sake** but **a record of lineage, history, and spiritual power**.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- The I1 unit is the **entire Northwest Coast cultural area** — the **narrow coastal strip** from **Yakutat Bay** (Alaska) to **Cape Mendocino** (California), plus the **adjacent river valleys** (the **Nass**, **Skeena**, **Fraser**, **Columbia**, **Klamath**, etc.).
- Bounded by the **Indigenous units** (the **Plateau peoples** to the east; the **California peoples** to the south; the **Dene** to the northeast) and the **settler units** (the **Left Coast** to the south; the **British Columbia coast** to the north).

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- The Indigenous population of the Pacific Northwest Coast is **hundreds of thousands today** (the exact number is given in the scope file as part of the "First Nations" listing).
- The peoples are **politically organized** into **band/tribe/nation** governments (the **Heiltsuk**, **Kwakwaka'wakw**, **Nuu-chah-nulth**, **Haida**, **Coast Salish**, **Gitxsan**, **Nisga'a**, etc.), with **land claims** and **self-government** agreements (the **Nisga'a Final Agreement**, the **Haida Gwaii** negotiations, the **Coast Salish** negotiations) being a **major political feature** of the region.
- The **Indigenous population** is **concentrated in the coastal towns** (Vancouver, Victoria, Seattle, Portland, Juneau) and **reservations** (the **Stó:lō**, **Tsawwassen**, **Squamish**, **Lummi**, **Nisga'a**, **Haida**).

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With the settler units:** The Northwest Coast's Indigenous peoples are **overlaid** by the **settler units** (the **British Columbia coast** and the **Left Coast**), but the **seams** are **not clean lines** — the **coastal towns** (Vancouver, Victoria, Seattle, Portland) are **Indigenous-majority or Indigenous-present**, and the **reservations** are **co-located** with the **settler towns** (the **Cedar** and **Coho** reservations adjacent to **Bellingham**, the **Salish Sea** reservations adjacent to **Seattle**).
- **With the Plateau peoples (I2):** The **Columbia Plateau** peoples (the **Nez Perce**, **Umatilla**, **Wallawalla**, **Klamath**, **Modoc**) are **adjacent** to the **Northwest Coast** (the **Chinookan** and **Sahaptin** speakers) and **share** the **salmon-based culture** but differ in **language** and **subsistence** (the **Plateau** peoples relied more on **roots** and **trade** with the plains).
- **With the California peoples (I3):** The **Yurok**, **Karuk**, **Hupa**, **Wiyot**, **Tolowa**, and **Chilula** are **adjacent** to the **Northwest Coast** (the **southernmost expression** of the culture area, per **Kroeber**) and **share** the **plank houses**, **dugout canoes**, **salmon fishing**, and **ceremonial cycles** but differ in **language** (the **Yurok** and **Wiyot** are **Algic**, related to **Algonquian**; the **Karuk** and **Hupa** are **Hokan** and **Athabaskan**, respectively).

## Snags and Self-corrections
- Initial reading treated the **Northwest Coast** and the **Plateau** as **separate** and **distinct**. Corrected: they are **adjacent** and **overlapping** in **subsistence** (both rely on **salmon**), but differ in **language** (Salishan, Sahaptin, Kutenai, Chinookan) and **subsistence emphasis** (the **Plateau** peoples relied more on **roots** and **trade** with the **plains**; the **Northwest Coast** peoples relied more on **salmon and marine mammals**).
- The **potlatch** was **suppressed** (1884–1951) but **not eliminated** — the **culture did not die**; the **revival** is **ongoing** (the **contemporary Northwest Coast art renaissance**, the **renewed** **expression of crest-bearing** and **lineage identity**).
