## Findings
- Cascadian values are grounded in bioregionalism and environmental stewardship. The Core Principles of the Cascadia Department of Bioregion explicitly list "Environmental Sustainability and Bioregionalism" as a core value, with the belief that "political boundaries should match ecological and cultural boundaries" and that "culture stems from place." The movement emphasizes "a philosophy called bioregionalism, which means breaking down and moving away from national boundaries and borders and shifting towards a more holistic approach which accurately reflects the physical realities of our watersheds."

- The Cascadia movement also values independence and inter-dependence. Core principles include "An Independent Cascadia Bioregion" with "a shared affinity with those living here that stems from this place we live, a ruggedly independent spirit, and a belief in equality and equity." The movement also values "Dynamic and Open Governance" with "local democracy, decentralizing existing centralized power structures."

- Community resilience and empowerment are valued. The Core Principles list "Community Resilience and Empowerment" with "self reliance, sustainability, networking and locally based disaster preparedness." The movement emphasizes "connections between the individual, the community and the bioregion which increase the ability of each to support itself."

- Indigenous sovereignty is a valued principle. The Core Principles list "Indigenous Sovereignty" as a core value, reflecting the recognition of tribal nations' rights and stewardship practices in the Pacific Northwest.

- Social values such as equity and inclusion are also prized. The Cascadia Partners organization lists "Equity, Sustainability, Pragmatism" as core principles, with "equity" being "as valuable and necessary for achieving equitable outcomes as the outcomes themselves." The Cascadia.edu college values include "equity and inclusion," "collaboration," "access," "success," "innovation," "environmental sustainability," "global awareness," "responsiveness," and "creativity."

- The Cascadia Consulting Group's values include "Community, Connection, Innovation, Sustainability, Quality," reflecting a business-oriented expression of Cascadian values.

- Communities across the Northwest "pride themselves on their environmental values and actions, such as promoting conservation or voluntarily employing sustainable practices." The Fifth National Climate Assessment notes that climate change has "disrupted sense of place in the Northwest, affecting noneconomic values such as proximity and access to nature and residents' feelings of security and stability." This suggests that environmental values are deeply tied to Cascadians' sense of place and well-being.

- The region's identity as an "ecotopia" reflects a cultural value on environmentalism. The Portland State University article notes that the Pacific Northwest became "nationally known as an 'ecotopia' and increasingly as a location of industrial nostalgia" with "consistent environmental content" in regional identity.

- Values violations in Cascadia would be perceived as actions that harm the environment, undermine community resilience, or violate Indigenous sovereignty. The bioregional movement emphasizes "responsibility to the well-being of the region" and "self-reliance, economic democracy, and responsibility to the whole web of life." This suggests that actions that harm the ecological community or the region's social fabric would be socially disapproved of.
