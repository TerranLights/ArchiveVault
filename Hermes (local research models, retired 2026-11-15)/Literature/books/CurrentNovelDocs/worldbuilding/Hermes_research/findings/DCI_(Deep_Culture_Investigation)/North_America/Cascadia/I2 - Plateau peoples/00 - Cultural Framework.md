## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- The **Plateau** peoples' territories were **occupied since the **last glacial maximum** (c. **15,000–12,000 years ago**), with the **Columbia-Fraser Plateau** being a **core area** of **human occupation** since the **Pleistocene**.
- The **cultural area** is the **high plateau region** between the **Rocky Mountains** (east) and the **Cascade Range** and **Canadian Coast Ranges** (west), covering parts of **Montana**, **Idaho**, **Oregon**, **Washington**, and **British Columbia**.
- The **peoples** (linguistic families): **Salishan** (the **Interior Salish** — the **Shuswap**, **Thompson**, **Lillooet**, **Northern Okanagan**, **Kalispel**, **Spokane**, **Coeur d'Alene**, **Sanpoil-Nespelem**, **Lakes**, **Flathead**, **Pend d'Oreille**, and **Nez Perce**), **Sahaptin** (the **Nimiipuu** (Nez Perce), **Umatilla**, **Walla Walla**, **Waptailmim** (Yakama), and the **Sahaptin** speakers of the **Columbia Plateau**), and **Kutenai** (an **isolate language** family).
- The **plateau** peoples are **defined by** a **high reliance on roots** (the **biscuitroot** and **camás**) as a **food source**, a **high reliance on short-duration salmon and eel runs**, and **long-term habitation of winter villages** at **fixed locations** along **rivers or lakes**.

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- The **Plateau** culture is **defined by** a **hybrid of **plenty** and **trade**: the **salmon** runs (short-duration, **prompting migration** to **fishing camps**), the **root crops** (the **camás** and **biscuitroot**), and the **trade networks** that **stretched across** the **plateau** and into the **plains** and **coastal** regions.
- The **culture** is **not as dense** as the **Northwest Coast** (no **potlatch**-based **ranked society**), but it is **more complex** than the **Great Basin** peoples (who had **no fixed villages** or **fixed territory**); the **Plateau** peoples **lived in** **winter villages** and **summer fishing camps**, with a **seasonal round** that **depended heavily on** the **salmon runs** and the **root crops**.
- The **governing frame** is **kinship-based**: **bands**, **clans**, and **lineages** (less ranked than the **Northwest Coast**), with a **focus on** **reciprocity**, **gift-giving**, and **trade** (the **trade networks** that **stretched** from the **Coast** to the **Plains**); the **trade goods** included **salt**, **salmon**, **furs**, **obsidian**, and **copper**.
- The **culture** is **spiritually rich**: the **spiritual** world is **defined** by **dreams**, **vision quests**, and **shamans** (the **medicine men** and **women** who **communicated** with the **spirit world**); the **ceremonies** were **focused on** **hunting and fishing success**, **healing**, and **survival**.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- The I2 unit is the **Columbia-Fraser Plateau** and the **inland basins** of the **Pacific Northwest** — the **high plateau region** between the **Rocky Mountains** and the **Cascade Range**, covering parts of **British Columbia**, **Washington**, **Oregon**, and **Idaho**.
- Bounded by the **Northwest Coast peoples** (to the west), the **Great Basin edge peoples** (to the south), the **Dene peoples** (to the northeast), and the **settler units** (the **Left Coast** and the **Far West**).

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- The **Indigenous population** of the **Plateau** is **hundreds of thousands** today (the exact number is given in the scope file as part of the "First Nations" listing).
- The peoples are **politically organized** into **band/tribe/nation** governments (the **Nez Perce**, **Umatilla**, **Walla Walla**, **Klamath**, **Modoc**, **Coeur d'Alene**, **Spokane**, **Kalispel**, **Sanpoil**, **Lakes**, **Flathead**, **Pend d'Oreille**, **Shuswap**, **Thompson**, **Lillooet**, **Okanagan**, **Secwepemc**, **Nlaka'pamux**, and **Sinixt**), with **land claims** and **self-government** agreements being a **major political feature** of the region.
- The **Indigenous population** is **concentrated** in the **reservations** and **rural communities** (the **Nez Perce** reservation in **Idaho**, the **Umatilla** reservation in **Oregon**, the **Yakama** reservation in **Washington**, the **Colville** reservation in **Washington**, the **Secwepemc** reserves in **BC**).

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With the Northwest Coast peoples (I1):** The **Columbia** and **Fraser** rivers are the **connection** — the **Chinookan** speakers of the **lower Columbia** (the **Wasco**, **Wishram**, **Klickitat**, **Kittitas**, **Palouse**, **Molala**) are **adjacent** to the **Northwest Coast** (the **Coast Salish** and **Chinookan**), and the **plateau** peoples **traded** with the **coastal** peoples (the **salmon**, **salt**, **furs**, and **obsidian** trade).
- **With the Great Basin edge peoples (I5):** The **Plateau** peoples **border** the **Great Basin** at the **southern edge** (the **Klamath** and **Modoc** peoples are **adjacent** to the **Owens Valley Paiute** and the **Northern Paiute**), and they **share** a **dependency on** **root crops** and **salmon**.
- **With the settler units (S1, S3):** The **Plateau** peoples are **overlaid** by the **settler units** (the **Left Coast** and the **Far West**), but the **seams** are **not clean lines** — the **reservations** are **co-located** with the **settler towns** (the **Nez Perce** reservation adjacent to **Lewiston**, the **Yakama** reservation adjacent to **Yakima**), and the **Indigenous** population is **present** in the **urban centers** (Seattle, Portland, Spokane).
