## Findings

### Founding Population and When (DCI line 01)
- Sudcalifornia's first settled layer was the **Jesuit mission colony** (1697–1840): the **Jesuits** (expelled in 1767) were the **first European institution** in the region, founding **Misión de Nuestra Señora de Loreto Conchó** in 1697 and a chain of missions (Santa Gertrudis, San Borja, Santa María) along the peninsula.
- The missions were **not just religious** but **agrarian and military** — they were the **primary institution** of Spanish colonization of Baja California.
- After the **Peninsular War** and the **Mexican War of Independence**, the mission system ended; the **ranchos** (land grants) were given to **Mexican settlers**; the **Yaqui** and **Mayo** and other **Indigenous groups** (the **Cochimí**, **Pericú**, **Guaycura** — hunter-gatherers with pottery but no agriculture or metalworking) were **missionized** and then **decimated** by disease.
- The **modern era** brought the **border economy** (Tijuana as the largest city, the **maquiladora** factories, tourism along the road), **mining** (baryte, other minerals), and **the Pacific Road** (the coastal highway that connected the region to the rest of Mexico).

### The Governing Frame (DCI line 02)
- The Sudcalifornia's governing frame is **the mission culture**, but **not the Californio ranch culture** (that is El Norte, S4):
    - **Sierra Pinal** (the **municipality** of **Sierra Pinal** is in the **Mexican state of Chihuahua**, not Baja California; the scope's "Sierra Pinal" entry likely refers to a fictional subdivision of Sudcalifornia, and no real-world place of that name exists in Baja California Sur).
    - The **Jesuit mission zone** is the **defining layer**: the **founding institution** (1697–1840), the **agrarian and military** structure, and the **spiritual framework** (Catholic Franciscan and Jesuit networks).
    - The **modern economy** is **resource-extraction and border trade** (mining, the road, the maquiladoras), which is more **pragmatic and extractive** than the Californio ranch culture, and sits in the **intervening space** between the **El Norte** (the Californian province) and the **Far West** (the desert).
- The culture is **a hybrid of the Jesuit mission-era and the modern border economy**, with a **shamanic and Franciscan** spiritual framework and a **younger, more aggressive, more masculine** rowdy streak (the "cowboy" and "miner" cultures) alongside the **mission's moral order**.

### Territory Within the Target (DCI line 03)
- Sudcalifornia is the **northern two-thirds of the Baja California peninsula** — from the **Pacific coast** up to the **Gulf of California** and the **Colorado River delta**.
- Bounded by the **Pacific Ocean** (west), the **Gulf of California** (east), the **U.S. state of California** (north, across the border), and the **northern third of the peninsula** (the **Ensenada** and **Tijuana** municipalities, which are in El Norte, S4).
- The **interior desert** (the **Vaca Mountains**, the **Sierra de San Francisco** with their **rock art**) is a **cultural seam** — the **pre-Hispanic** and **mission-era** landscape is preserved but **not inhabited** by the modern border economy.

### Present-Day Demographics (DCI line 04)
- **3.5M+** population (including El Norte's Tijuana).
- The population is **concentrated in the coastal strip**: **Tijuana** (the largest city, 2.0M metro), **Mexicali** (1.0M), **Ensenada** (0.3M), **San Felipe** (on the Gulf), **San Quintín** (the agricultural center), and the **towns of the mission zone** (Loreto, Santa Gertrudis, San Borja, Santa María).
- The **interior desert** (the **Cabo** region) is **sparsely populated** (no more than a few thousand permanent residents); the **Cabo San Lucas** resort town is a **tourist enclave**.
- The **Indigenous population** (Cochimí, Pericú, Guaycura survivors) is **small** but **politically organized** (the **Comquier** and the **Baja California State Indigenous Council**).

### Seams (DCI line 05)
- **With El Norte (S4):** The seam is the **historic Californian province border** — the **Palóu Line** and the **Californio border**; the two units share the **Spanish-Mexican framework** but differ in **jurisdiction** (U.S./Mexican) and **mission vs. ranch wealth** (El Norte's ranch wealth is in the **Rancho** system; Sudcalifornia's is in the **mission system**).
- **With the Far West (S3):** The seam is the **desert–agriculture boundary** — the **Pacific Road** (the coastal highway) connecting the **Minatitlán and Caborca** mining towns with the **Central Valley** of California; the **Colorado River delta** is a **cultural seam** (the **Cocopah** and **Kumeyaay** live on both sides).
- **With the Indigenous units:** The **Cochimí, Pericú, and Guaycura** are the **original inhabitants**; the **Jesuit missions** were the **first European institution**; the **modern border economy** is the **latest layer**.
