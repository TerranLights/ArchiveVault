## The author's note on "particulars of regional standards" (2026-11-25, verbatim)

Additionally, I should explain what I mean by "particulars of regional standards". To provide some examples, when Christopher Columbus discovered North America, he mistakenly referred to as the autochtonic, aboriginal people of North America as "Indians". Naturally, this is obviously wrong, but the name still stays. In the 1980s, a journalist once referred to the particular style of Heavy Metal coming from England as the quote, New Wave of British Heavy Metal, which then got abbreviated to NWOBHM. This is very easily the singular clumsiest abbreviation ever recorded in human history, and yet the name still stays. I once saw a writer describe this phenomenon in the sense that, as he put it, "Whoever names it first, usually names it for good".

What I'm getting at, in terms of my own Tepenian universe, is how the regional cultures of the various geographical regions of the Tepenian Federation establish norms that become commonly-known standard practice throughout the entire country. One ready, in-universe example of this is Comms. Tepenia's radio and communication infrastructure was first built and established by Australians. Therefore, Australian Comms protocol eventually goes on to become the official national protocol. Indeed, many vocabulary terms in the national lexicon that are oriented towards and or related to communications, radio networks, and related infrastructure derives its origins from Australian slang. Just the same, much of Tepenia's radio and communications protocol derives its origins from Australian protocol.

So, when I say "particulars of regional standards", what I mean by that is, for each regional culture, for each subnet, for each area, which aspects of Tepenian national culture are derived from that particular regional culture? This is a complex series of topics that will take a long time to determine.

**Starting points for when this item begins** (the author asked for this note, 2026-11-25: "those are definitely important, and they can definitely be used"):

- **Which culture established which thing.** The question is about *things*, not cities (author, 2026-11-25: "this is not about 'who founded which city'. This is about which particular cultures established which *things*"): for each system, institution, practice or norm of the national culture (communications, as in the Australian example, and so on), which regional culture established it first, and what of that culture it carried into the national standard. No list of these exists yet; the Australian Comms example is so far recorded only in the author's note above. Each case is a derivation the author decides.
- **The cultures doing the shaping.** The full-depth DCI research on each contributing culture (Culture Library, `Reference/CultureLibrary/`) shows what each one brought with it, and so what a standard it set would carry. The later DCI synthesis of each city's own local culture is where a regional culture's standards and the national ones meet.
- **The vocabulary trail.** `Reference/slang-synthesis/` and `Reference/TepenianLanguage/` are where words like the Australian radio slang get traced into the national lexicon.

## Rules for every session

1. **Read this file first** when the author works on one of the items, and the item's own files after it (each repo's own rules and canon still apply there).
2. **Small pieces.** Help the author pick and finish **one** small piece. Never pile more onto an item than a day holds, and never turn the list into a backlog to clear. Small daily wins are the whole point.

4. **Stages are the author's.** Propose a stage change in the log; change the Stage column only when the author agrees.
5. **Parallel sessions.** Several sessions may use this file at once. Re-read it right before you edit it. Change only your own item's row. Add to the log only at the end, and never rewrite or reorder anyone else's lines. When committing (with the author's OK), stage and commit only your own rows and lines; leave other sessions' uncommitted edits in the working tree for them.
6. **Don't duplicate the work here.** This file records where things stand and points to where the work lives. The work itself stays in its own repo or folder.
7. **American English**, plain words. The author's standing rules apply as everywhere else.
