# Ilse Varga: Doorway #1, Midpoint, Doorway #2 (MADE-UP EXAMPLE)

**Not the beats themselves.** Tags: see `README.md`.

**Working from (established):** Enneagram Type Six, adjusted to her (her version: she trusts a person's judgment over her own until that person fails her, then trusts no one). Defense: projection. Flaw: hands her decisions to whoever seems to know best. Greatest fear: being left without anyone to steer by. Goal: keep the workshop open through the winter. Ghost: her father lost a first workshop after trusting a patron who cheated him. Archetype arc: one, the Hero Arc. Setting: a mountain town, snowed in from the second month.

**Core structural difficulty (Step 1.4):** a Six's characteristic failure is handing the choice to someone else, so both Doorways, which are choices only she can make, are the hardest beats in the book for her.

---

## Doorway #1 (~20%, ~p. 60)

### MUST
1. **Ilse makes the choice herself.** Whatever the event, the decision to cross is hers, not an order or a request she merely obeys [Composite; Bell].
2. **The choice goes against the advice of someone she trusts.** This is the first time she overrules a trusted voice; anything less leaves her flaw untouched [CDM].
3. **The door closes behind her.** After it, the old arrangement with the patron cannot be restored on any terms [Snyder; Bell].
4. **The cost is the town's approval, not money alone.** For a Six, losing the group's backing is the real threshold [CDM; Proposed].

### CANNOT
1. **A third party's act standing as the crossing.** No letter, eviction or accident may do the crossing for her [Composite].
2. **Her fear tested here.** Being left with no one to steer by belongs to the Midpoint and later; testing it now spends it too early [CDM].
3. **A coincidence that opens the way.** Only the Catalyst may be a coincidence [Law].

---

## Midpoint / Mirror Moment (50%, ~p. 150)

### MUST (whatever shape is chosen)
1. **The stakes rise and the winter's clock is felt.** The time left before the workshop's debts fall due becomes concrete [Snyder].
2. **Ilse sees herself as she is.** The Mirror Moment asks the question her flaw has kept her from asking: whose judgment has she been living by [Bell; CDM]?
3. **It turns on the town's opposition, not on the weather.** The winter is the setting; the opponent is the people who want the workshop closed [Truby 7].

### CANNOT
1. **Her problem solved.** A Midpoint that resolves the debt ends the story's engine [Snyder].
2. **A new mentor appearing to guide her.** That would feed the flaw instead of exposing it [CDM].

### Menu of shapes (options, not a recommendation)
- **False victory:** a large order arrives. *Additionally requires:* the order hides a cost she will discover at All Is Lost.
- **False defeat:** the furnace cracks. *Additionally requires:* the loss is traceable to a choice she handed to someone else.
- **Mirror Moment alone:** she is left with a finished piece and no buyer. *Additionally requires:* the scene is private, so the realization is hers, not explained to her.

---

## Doorway #2 (~80%, ~p. 240)

### MUST
1. **She chooses with no one to steer by.** Her greatest fear is now present, and she chooses anyway [CDM; Bell].
2. **The choice answers All Is Lost.** It is her solution to the low point, not a reaction to a new attack [Composite].
3. **The method is one she has practiced on the page.** Her solution uses a skill or knowledge shown earlier, so it is earned [Proposed].

### CANNOT
1. **A rescue by coincidence.** No lucky find, letter or buyer gets her out [Law].
2. **Another person's decision standing as hers** [Law].
